How it works

From one speed test to a map of India

Every shared test adds a little more detail. Here is what happens, and why some areas don't have a score yet.

Three steps

  1. 1
    You test your internet

    Your browser measures download, upload, response time and stability against a nearby M-Lab server.

  2. 2
    Your result joins a small area

    If you choose to share, your phone turns your location into a ~150 m square. Your exact position never leaves your device.

  3. 3
    Areas get a score

    Once enough people have tested, the area shows Good, Fair or Weak on the map.

The Connection Score

A single number out of 100 that balances what people feel most:

  • Download speed40%
  • Response time25%
  • Upload speed20%
  • Stability15%
Good 70–100 Fair 40–69 Weak 0–39

An area's score is the middle value (median) of its tests, so one unusual result can't swing it.

Why "Not enough tests"?

We only show a score when the data can fairly represent the place:

  • Block (~150 m) or neighbourhood (~1 km)5+ tests
  • City20+ tests, 3+ neighbourhoods
  • District30+ tests, 5+ neighbourhoods
  • State100+ tests across 3+ districts

"No tests yet" and "Weak" are never the same thing. Only tests from the last 6 months count.

Common questions

How do you know my provider?

Your internet address belongs to a network run by a company. We look that up in a database on our own server for a moment, then forget the address. If you use a VPN, we say so, and that result isn't counted in area scores.

Who runs the speed test?

Measurement Lab (M-Lab), a non-profit research platform. M-Lab publishes every test, including your IP address, as open data. You're told this before each test.

Can I test without sharing?

Yes. Leave "Add my result to the community map" off. You'll still see your full result, and nothing is sent to SpeedSpot.

Does a good score guarantee fast internet?

No. Scores are based on past tests. Networks change during the day and over time, so treat them as a helpful guide.
